## 1. Target and output

SHA-256 with 39 steps in every compression, FIPS 180-4 padding, the standard IV, feed-forward after step 38, all 256 digest bits. The output is two distinct 128-byte messages `M0 || M1` and `M0 || M1'`: the same first block, second blocks that give the same chaining value after the second compression; the padding block that follows is identical, so the two digests are equal. The first block `M0` is a random block, `cv = compress39(IV, M0)`.

## 2. The construction (sraiw 38912d66, dariolina)

**2.1 The base is advice.** The pair of ePrint 2026/1120, Table 23, is a 39-step semi-free-start collision: a chaining value `CV_PUB` (8 words), two blocks `M'` and `M` (16 words each), 160 bytes, printed in `experiments/r39joint.py` section [1] (`CV_PUB`, `M_PUB`, `MP_PUB`). It is a collision of the 39-step compression from `CV_PUB` (experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trial 0; the program's `compress39` equals the organizer's `verifier/hash_functions.py` on 300 random blocks, and the published pair collides under the organizer reference). It is the advice of this entry. Its trajectories `BA, BE` (message `M'`) and `CA, CE` (message `M`) are expanded from the 160 bytes by two compressions at the start of the run (charged 2 x 2812 + 120 operations, `Attack.base_setup`).

**2.2 The transplant.** Write `A[r]` and `E[r]` for the two state registers after step `r-1`, `a0 = A[4]`, `a1 = A[5]`, `u = W1`. Fix `A[6..9]`, `E[10..13]` and `W10..W15` to the published values of `M'`. For any chaining value `cv` and any `(a0, a1)`, steps 0 to 9 then determine `W0..W9` uniquely (`build(cv, a0, a1)` in section [1]); the partner message takes `W8 + D8`, `W9 + D9` and the published `W10..W15` of `M` (`second`). Both messages then follow the published trajectories `A[6..19]`, `E[10..19]` (steps 0 to 15 only, an identity of the step function, not a probability). The map `(cv, a0, a1) <-> (W8, W9, W16..W23)` is a bijection (320 bits, triangular). Experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trials 1 to 255, checks the identity on 255 random chaining values.

**2.3 Row 16 is a claw.** `W16 = sigma1(W14) + W9 + sigma0(W1) + W0`. With `a1 = u + h(a0, cv)` (`recover_a1`) one finds `W16 - g = F(u) - F(a0)` (mod 2^32), where `g = 0xbca1c321` is the published `W16`, `F(x) = F_even(x)` for even `x` (a 32-bit expression in `x`, `S0(x)`, `MAJ`, `S1`, `CH` and six constants of the first block: `consts`, `fstep`) and `F(x) = sigma0(x) - x` for odd `x`. So `W16 = g` exactly when `F(a0) = F(u)` with `a0` even and `u` odd: a claw of the map `F` on 32-bit words (checked in `r39-transplant-claw`, `w16_identity`). Claws are found by a van Oorschot-Wiener walk (Section 3).

**2.4 What remains to pay, after a claw.** (i) `W8 in F8` and `W9 in F9`, where `F8 = { w : sigma0(w + D8) - sigma0(w) = 0x087ff000 }`, `F9 = { w : sigma0(w + D9) - sigma0(w) = 0x03010000 }` (`inF`); both sets depend only on 14 and 17 active bits of `w`, with exactly 1152 / 2^14 = 9/128 and 2080 / 2^17 = 65/4096 members (enumerated; the active-bit masks `0xCC19E60C` and `0xF9983C0F`), so `p8 p9 = 585 / 524288 = 2^-9.807707`. (ii) `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`, the published `W17` (the program tests it on every claw): probability 2^-32 for a uniform `W17`; the entry needs only that this value works (`solve_row_exact` in `r39-transplant-claw` trial 0 verifies `|G16| = 2`, `|G17(0xbca1c121)| = 0`, and `|G17(g)| = 1` with `G17(g) = {0x71e9ba98}`). (iii) The tail: rows 18 to 38 (`W18..W23` are the six free words of the bijection) must end with equal states after step 38: probability `p_tail`, Section 8.

Events (i) to (iii), together with `W16 = g` and the parity `a0` even, `u` odd, imply the collision: the pair then follows the published trail through row 17 and its remaining rows hold with the tail event. The program ends by compressing the pair and comparing (`Attack.claw`, last block).

## 3. The program

One file, `experiments/r39joint.py` (sections [1] to [5]; the generator source is the string `GEN_C`, printed by `--gen-c`). Per first block `M0` the program runs one WINDOW of 2^26 walk evaluations (`Attack.run_window`):

1. Two random 256-bit words give `M0`; one target compression gives `cv`; the six constants of `F` (`consts`, 140 operations).
2. The generator `GEN_C` (freestanding C, compiled to WebAssembly, Appendix B) runs inside the program and emits the bank A to bank B program of the 256-lane walk step, about 723 records of 5 bytes, with the five constants of the first block folded in; the bank B to bank A program is the same records with the bank bits of every register field swapped (a relocation pass, 8 operations per record). A first block whose image has more than 780 records, needs more than 206 registers, or whose generator runs over 575,000 operators is skipped and charged (`Attack.window_setup`). The executed-operator count of `GEN_C` is the full cost of producing the window's kernel: the construction of the data-flow graph, the register allocation, and the emission of the 5-byte records are each steps of `GEN_C`, and every one of its operators is charged (Section 4), inside `m.o` and the work budget of Section 7. The Python routine `make_kernels_folded` of section [1] is only a readable reference that produces byte-identical records (Appendix B: 200 first blocks, both banks; the program asserts the equality in check mode); its `to_source`/`exec` step (compiling a Python function) has no counterpart in the machine model, which executes the 5-byte records directly, charged per record per iteration in stages 1 and 2 (steps 4-5). No construction step is left uncharged.
3. The table is cleared (4 operations per 32-byte word); 256 trails start in the 256 lanes, trail `i` at `x0 = i`.
4. Stage 1 (`Attack.iter1`): 2^18 iterations of the kernel (one record = XOR, AND, OR or NOT of two 256-bit registers into a third: bit `j` of the 256 words of the lanes lives in plane `j`, rotations are renaming). A distinguished point (DP) is a new point whose low 7 bits are zero; the kernel ends with the OR of the low 7 planes. On a DP (`Attack.hit`): the lane is found (binary search); the DP lanes of the iteration are grouped (change (6)) and the 25 high bits `r` of all lanes of a group are extracted by one Horner pass over the planes with the group's mask; the bucket is `k = floor(r * 171008 / 2^25)` (`171008 = 668 x 2^8`, `668 = 2^9 + 2^7 + 2^5 - 2^2`: four shifts, two adds and a subtract; change (9)) and its 39-bit record `chk(8) | len(11) | id(20)`, `chk = r mod 256`, starts at bit `39 k` of the 26,052-word table (a record that crosses a word boundary takes a second load and, when written, a second store; this entry's change (3)); a bucket holds at most 197 consecutive values of `r`, so (bucket, chk) determines `r` and there is no false match. The record is overwritten except when a MATCH finds a stored trail at least as long as the new one (keep-longer, change (1)), and a hit that does not MATCH writes only if its trail has at least `LMIN = 32` steps (short-trail skip, change (4)); `check` mode asserts `len < 2048` and the bucket formula on every hit. An entry with the same `chk` and a nonzero length is a MATCH (two trails end at one DP): the pair of (id, len) goes into a queue of 3,584 pairs. The lane receives a new trail (next id; the ids of a group's lanes are written plane by plane by one shared pass that XORs each lane's 20-bit delta against its known value `r x 2^7` into planes 0 to 19 and clears planes 20 to 31, changes (6) and (8)). A trail older than 1536 steps is cut by a scan every 512 iterations (`MAXS = 1536`, `CUTP = 512`), so every trail is shorter than 2,048 steps.
5. Stage 2 (`Attack.stage2`), when the queue holds QFULL = 3328 pairs and at the end of the window (an iteration pushes at most one pair per lane, so the queue never holds more than 3327 + 256 = 3583 < QCAP = 3584 pairs: a constant inequality, no probability): pairs run in lane pairs `(p, p + 128)` (128 slots, continuous refill), and the longer trail of a pair whose length difference is at least `D0 = 82` first runs alone in any free lane (change (5): a 256-bit occupancy mask; a finishing catch-up whose partner lane is busy is moved, 4 operations per plane, into a fully free lane pair from a packed free-slot stack; launches that need a fully free lane pair wait while fewer than `KRES = 6` are free; with none free the pair is dropped). When every queued pair has been launched, a call that is not the window's last one parks (this entry's change (2)): the in-flight pairs stay in their lanes, the current stage-2 bank is swapped with the 32-word save area that holds the stage-1 bank (a counted loop of 32 x (2 loads + 2 stores + 3 loop) + 3 = 483 operations; it is the existing save area), the six scalars `cur`, `it2`, `bact`, `live`, `occ`, `nf` are stored (18 operations), and the next stage-2 call tests the parked flag (2), loads them (18) and swaps the banks back (483) and continues with the pairs where they were (a call that finds nothing parked saves the stage-1 bank, and a final call restores it: 291 operations each; a call that finds nothing parked also initializes the six scalars and the 4-word free-slot stack, 22 operations in block `s2_init`, and every call sets its queue cursors, 3 operations in block `s2_q`; the timing wheel is empty after every drain, which check mode asserts, so it is never re-zeroed, and a lane record is always written before it is read); the timing wheel (2,048 buckets: a pair's length difference is at most 2,047), the lane records and the free-slot stack stay as they are. The last call of a window (after the 2^18 iterations) runs until every pair has merged. The longer trail starts at its start point, the shorter one `len` difference iterations later (a timing wheel); after each iteration the two states of a pair are compared (12 planes first, the other 20 only for candidates); the first iteration with equal states gives the predecessors `(a, b)`. Opposite parity is a claw (`a0` even, `u` odd). Every lane insert of stage 2 is queued and the queue is written by one shared pass (change (7)) before any lane move, kernel step, park or drain.
6. Claw filters (`Attack.claw`, exact, scalar, every operation counted): `a1 = sigma0(u) + a0 + const` (uses the claw equation), `W9 = c9 - a1 in F9`; then `W8 in F8`; then `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`. A claw that passes all three is built into the pair `(M1, M1')`; both are compressed (2 target compressions, charged) and compared; a collision ends the run.

The attack is the loop over first blocks. In `--attack` the 16 words of each window's first block are drawn from the operating system's random source (`random.SystemRandom`, i.e. `os.urandom`): fresh algorithmic coins for every window, so the windows are independent trials of the algorithm (H1 part (a) is about the hash, not about the coins); `--seed S` exists only to make the experiments and the `--windows` measurements reproducible and is not used by the program. Its halts are the window count `B` and the charged-work budget `W_BUDGET` (Section 7), a skipped image (charged), and success; if a window's trail counter `idc` reaches 2^20 (trail ids are 20 bits), the `Attack.hit` or `Attack.cutscan` branch that finds no free id sets the stage-1 limit register to 0 (1 operation, on that path only; no per-iteration test is added), so stage 1 ends after the current iteration and the window's final stage 2 drains the queue and any parked pairs (`r39-window-small` trial 4 forces this path; across the 73 full windows `idc` peaks at 521,533 to 527,529, mean 524,309.4, under 0.504 x 2^20).

## 4. Machine model and prices (declared once)

A counted word RAM of 256-bit words with 256 registers (8,192 bytes, counted as memory), the cost model collision-frontier-v5: the unit is the target compression, 2812 operations (`C` of `sha256-r39`). Charged, one operation each: XOR, AND, OR, NOT, move, shift, rotate, add, subtract, compare and set; a conditional branch is a compare and a branch (2); a load or a store is 3 (index shift, base add, access); a call and its return are 2. A main-loop body record (register-to-register XOR, AND, OR, NOT, no immediate operand) is 5 bytes (an opcode and three 10-bit register fields), the price of dariolina's 6b678689 and of Jbenisek's 0c1ef7c3; every other instruction is 36 bytes. One executed WebAssembly operator of the generator is one charged operation (generator halts at 575,000 operators); this count is the whole kernel-construction cost -- the data-flow graph build, the register allocation and the 5-byte record emission performed by `GEN_C` -- and not a surrogate for it, since `GEN_C` and the Python reference `make_kernels_folded` emit identical records (Appendix B). These are prices of the cost model; they are not probabilistic premises. Every operation of the program goes through the class `Mach` of the program (one method = one charged primitive, `m.o` counts them); there is no averaged price anywhere in the ledger.

## 7. Success probability and time

`p_claw = p8 p9 2^-32 p_tail`, `log2 p_claw` = -117.247707 (with `log2 p_tail` = -75.44, Section 8). **Claws are counted by groups.** Two claws of a window can depend on each other only if they share `a0` or `u` (a value with three or more preimages under `F`); grouping the claws of a window into connected components by shared endpoint (`count_groups`), one claw per group is enough for the event, and distinct groups have disjoint inputs `(a0, u)` of the bijection. Over the 73 full windows (Appendix A), there are `4,303,243` distinct claw groups in `4,309,745` distinct claws: per-window mean `58948.53` groups (standard deviation `251.73`, one-sided 99% Student-t lower bound `g- = 58,878.41` groups/window) and group-to-claw ratio `4,303,243 / 4,309,745 = 0.998491` (per-window mean `0.998491`, standard deviation `0.000154`, one-sided 99% Student-t lower bound `r- = 0.998448`, giving `c- r- = 58,967.19 x 0.998448 = 58,875.67 <= g-`). Conditional on producing at least `G` distinct groups, the run succeeds with probability `1 - (1 - p_claw)^G >= 1 - exp(-G p_claw)`. To absorb the Hoeffding failure probability with an explicit positive margin above `0.39`, we set `G p_claw = lambda = -ln(0.60999) = 0.494313`, so `1 - exp(-G p_claw) = 0.390010`, `log2 G` = 116.2312, and with the 99% lower bound `r- = 0.998448`, `N = G / r- = 2^116.2334` distinct claws. Over the distinct claws (the trials the claim counts), the F9 and F8 counts are binomial from window to window (chi-square 91.3 and 86.6 on 72 degrees of freedom against the pooled empirical rates, 91.5 and 88.7 against the exact rates 65/4096 and 9/128; pooled 68,460 / 4,309,745 = 0.015885 against 65/4096 = 0.015869, z = 0.3, which the claim does not use (it takes the exact 65/4096, premise (c)), and 4,879 / 68,460 = 0.07127 against 9/128 = 0.07031, z = 1.0); counted over raw claws they are over-dispersed (chi-square 119.6 and 136.7), because a duplicate raw claw repeats the `(W8, W9)` of its distinct claw. The lag-1 autocorrelation of the F9 indicator over the distinct claws in discovery order is within 0.0092 in every window (claw-weighted mean 0.0002; 1099 adjacent F9 pairs against 1087.5 expected under independence, z = 0.3; this count is not used by the claim: a pairwise dependence between claws enters the success probability only through joint terms of order `G p_claw^2 ~ 2^-117`, which are negligible; it is one of about 15 such tests, and a deficit of z = -2.5 seen once during development, on windows 140000 to 140072 of a walk with different bookkeeping (996 against 1,078.2), did not reproduce on fresh windows (holdout seeds 555001 to 555005: 74 against 69.8)). Amplification is over distinct claw groups, so the window count uses the direct per-window group endpoint `g- = 58,878.41` (0xplemonade's 69616879 refinement, credited; the product path `c- r- = 58,875.67` is no longer used). With the margin `(1 + 2^-19)`, `B = floor(G (1 + 2^-19) / g-) + 1 = 1656240204330672542645907095553 = 2^100.3858` windows (first blocks), and the Hoeffding bounds of this section hold with `g-` in place of `c- r-`.

**Total work and total groups/claws (Hoeffding).** The windows are independent trials (fresh coins per window, H1 part (a)), each with charged work in `[0, R]`, distinct claws in `[0, 2^26]`, and distinct claw groups in `[0, 2^26]`. By Hoeffding's inequality the total work of `B` windows exceeds `B mu + B mu+ 2^-20` (and so in particular `B mu+ (1 + 2^-20)`, as `mu <= mu+`) with probability at most `exp(-2 B (mu+ 2^-20)^2 / R^2) <= exp(-2^17.1)`, the total number of distinct claws falls below `B c (1 - 2^-20) >= N` with probability at most `exp(-2^40.9)`, and the total number of distinct claw groups falls below `B g (1 - 2^-20) >= B g- (1 - 2^-20) >= B c- r- (1 - 2^-20) >= G` with probability at most `exp(-2^40.9)` (`mu, c, g` the true means; `mu <= mu+`, `c >= c-` and `g >= g-` is premise H1 part (b)). So with probability at least `1 - 2 exp(-2^17.1)` all `B` windows run inside the budget and produce at least `G` distinct claw groups (and at least `N` distinct claws).

**The budget.** `--attack` stops at a success, after `B_WINDOWS` windows, or as soon as its charged work reaches `W_BUDGET = floor(B x 492,864,583 x (1 + 2^-20)) + 1 = 816302916141654826192566491984431187486 = 2^129.2624` operations (checked before each window, 6 operations per window), whichever comes first, so its charged work is at most `W_BUDGET + R + 6 B` in every run. The time is

`T = (W_BUDGET + R + 6 B) / 2812` target-compressions (`log2(T + 2^36) = 117.80499`), `time_log2` = 117.805, against 117.7076 at the pooled tail mean -75.3426; by the union bound, the unconditional success probability is at least `(1 - 0.60999) - 2 exp(-2^17.1) = 0.390010 - 2 exp(-2^17.1) > 0.390009 > 0.39`.

## 9. Premises (declared in claim.json)

`H1-claw-model` (score-critical), four parts. (a) The chaining value `cv` of a random first block behaves as an independent random value for the walk map and the later events, so the windows are independent trials. (b) The program's yield and cost per window are those measured on the real step function over 73 full-size windows (59037.60 distinct claws, 58948.53 distinct claw groups, 8316.7 operations per claw, with one-sided 99% bounds `mu+ = 492,864,583`, `c- = 58,967.19`, `g- = 58,878.41`, `r- = 0.998448`); the program cannot be run for 2^100.3858 windows. (c) A distinct claw is, for the events `W8 in F8`, `W9 in F9`, `W17 = published` and the tail, a trial with the probabilities of Sections 2.4 and 8, independent of the walk's yield. (d) `W17` is uniform over the claws.

Test of (c) and (d), on claws of the real walk. First, directly in the organizer sandbox, `r39-window-small` trials 0..3 run 4 independent windows (`2^24` walk evaluations total, `3,270` distinct claws and `3,270` distinct claw groups, Section 10) and hard-assert `WIN_EXP` and `serve()`: `W9 in F9` 52 (51.9 expected), `W8 in F8` 228 (229.9 expected), joint `F9 & F8` 2 (3.65 expected), `W17` low-8-bit matches 11 (12.77 expected), low-12-bit matches 2 (0.80 expected), and 256-bin chi-square of `W17 mod 256` = `241.19` (255 df) and `W18 mod 256` = `246.36` (255 df). ## 11. Advice and preprocessing

Preprocessing (`H3-advice-construction`, the form of sraiw 38912d66 Section 4.2, credited). The program reads from the advice only the dense part of the published characteristic: the state words `a`, `e` of steps 2 to 15 of both blocks, the message words `W10..W15`, the differences `D8`, `D9`, the row-16 value `g` and the row-17 value `0x71e9ba98`; every word after row 17 is a fresh draw of the run, paid online by the 2^-32 of row 17 and by `p_tail` (Sections 2.4, 7 and 8). The cost of producing that dense part is counted and declared as `H3-advice-construction`: (1) The 6 steps 10 to 15 carry 12 state words (384 bits) under 350 bit, carry and message-schedule conditions of the Table 23 characteristic (38912d66 Section 4.2), leaving a space of 2^(384-350) = 2^34 local dense-base candidates on steps 10 to 15; once steps 10 to 15 and `W0..W15` are fixed, steps 9 down to 0 and `CV_PUB` are uniquely determined downward with zero search because each SHA-256 step is invertible (`invert_downward` in `r39joint.py`, verified in `r39-transplant-claw` trials 0 and 1: `downward_inv_ok = 1`). (2) Each candidate is tested for a non-empty row-16 fibre `G16` and row-17 fibre `G17(g)` by exact 32-bit bit-by-bit DFS (`solve_row_exact` in `r39joint.py`, using `ROW_MASKS_16_17` and `CONDS_16_17`). In `r39-transplant-claw` trials 0 and 1, `verify_advice_construction()` executes `solve_row_exact` on the published base (`g16_fibre_size = 2` with `G16 = 0xbca1c321`, `g17_fibre_size = 1` with `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`, `fibre_dfs_ops = 1944 < 2812` word operations = `< 1` target-compression) and enumerates a 256-candidate local slice of the step-15 free bits (`advice_slice_cands = 256`, `advice_slice_hits = 1`, `ops_per_cand = 408 < 4 * 2812` word operations = `< 2^2` target-compressions). Over all 2^34 candidates this is at most `2^34 x 2^2 = 2^36` target-compressions (`preprocessing_log2 = 36`). The paper's own figures agree: its Step 1 (a SAT search producing `W8..W13` and the states of steps 0 to 13) costs about 2^34.2 SHA-256 calls, and its Step 2 "holds with probability 1 since there are no conditions on (W5, W6, W7, E1, E2, E3)" (ePrint 2026/1120 Sect. 4.3). Memory of the enumeration: the DFS holds one candidate path at a time (fixed characteristic `< 2^12` bytes, one partial assignment `< 2^12` bytes, round state `< 2^10` bytes), at most `2^16` bytes, inside the `2^20`-byte arena of Section 6, so `memory_log2_bytes` stays 20. Score sensitivity: `2^117.80499 + 2^36` rounds up to `117.805`, and any preprocessing cost up to `2^98` target-compressions adds `< 2^-20` to `T` and leaves `time_log2 = 117.805` unchanged.

## Appendix B. The generator

With `R39_WASM=gen39b.wasm` (and the `wasmtime` package) the program charges the executed operators of each generation (wasmtime fuel, one operator = one operation): mean 523.6k, standard deviation 8.0k, maximum 543k over 200 first blocks; it emits records equal, line by line, to those of the Python generator `make_kernels_folded` of section [2] (200 first blocks, both banks).
