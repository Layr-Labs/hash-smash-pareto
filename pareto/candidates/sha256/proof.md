# SHA-256 reduced to 39 steps: ordinary collision by a transplanted advice pair, a row-16 claw search and a bit-sliced 256-lane walk, with cheap-kernel window selection and 7 x 2^24-evaluation windows

Target `sha256-r39-prefix-v1`. Claim: `time_log2` = 116.780 target-compressions, `memory_log2_bytes` = 20 (1,047,436 bytes, the 160-byte advice included), success probability at least 0.39, `preprocessing_log2` = 36 (Section 11, `H3-advice-construction`), advice 160 bytes.

Credit and what this entry changes. This entry is Jbenisek's 651ada7 (time_log2 = 116.865: dkuthoore's accepted walk program 0b2616e3 with 651ada7's second 39-step pair on the published characteristic as advice, Section 2.1, Appendix C) with two changes to how first blocks and windows are chosen, nothing else: (A) **cheap-kernel window selection**: the folded walk kernel of a first block has between about 660 and 760 records depending on the block's chaining value (mostly on `popcount(cv4 ^ cv5)`, the bits where the `CH` term of `F` depends on the walk point), and since a rejected first block costs only its compression, a 32-step popcount loop and (rarely) one charged generation, the program now draws first blocks until one passes a counted prefilter `8 popcount(cv4 ^ cv5) - 3 popcount(cv0 ^ cv1) <= 19` and its kernel image has at most `KCAP = 680` records (was 780); (B) **longer windows**: one window is `7 x 2^24` walk evaluations (was `2^26`), which amortizes the table warm-up of each window over more claws while keeping every trail id below `2^20` (peak 921,520 = 0.879 x 2^20). ## 1. Target and output

SHA-256 with 39 steps in every compression, FIPS 180-4 padding, the standard IV, feed-forward after step 38, all 256 digest bits. The output is two distinct 128-byte messages `M0 || M1` and `M0 || M1'`: the same first block, second blocks that give the same chaining value after the second compression; the padding block that follows is identical, so the two digests are equal. The first block `M0` is a random block, `cv = compress39(IV, M0)`.

## 2. The construction (sraiw 38912d66, dariolina)

**2.1 The base is advice: our second pair on the published characteristic.** The advice is our 39-step semi-free-start collision (`CV_PUB`, `M_PUB`, `MP_PUB` of `experiments/r39joint.py` section [1]; 160 bytes), printed with its signed characteristic and recompute in Appendix C. Its signed characteristic is the 39-step characteristic of ePrint 2026/1120 Tables 21 and 23 unchanged: every signed row, state and message schedule, is the published one, only the values differ, and `W16 = 0xbca1c321` and `W17 = 0x71e9ba98` are the published values. The state and message values are this pair's own, which is why the tail is re-measured on it (Section 8). It is a collision of the 39-step compression from `CV_PUB` (experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trial 0; `compress39` equals the organizer's `verifier/hash_functions.py` on 300 random blocks). Its trajectories `BA, BE` (`M'`) and `CA, CE` (`M`) are expanded from the 160 bytes by two compressions at the start of the run (charged 2 x 2812 + 120 operations, `Attack.base_setup`).

**2.2 The transplant.** Write `A[r]` and `E[r]` for the two state registers after step `r-1`, `a0 = A[4]`, `a1 = A[5]`, `u = W1`. Fix `A[6..9]`, `E[10..13]` and `W10..W15` to the published values of `M'`. For any chaining value `cv` and any `(a0, a1)`, steps 0 to 9 then determine `W0..W9` uniquely (`build(cv, a0, a1)` in section [1]); the partner message takes `W8 + D8`, `W9 + D9` and the published `W10..W15` of `M` (`second`). Both messages then follow the published trajectories `A[6..19]`, `E[10..19]` (steps 0 to 15 only, an identity of the step function, not a probability). The map `(cv, a0, a1) <-> (W8, W9, W16..W23)` is a bijection (320 bits, triangular). Experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trials 1 to 255, checks the identity on 255 random chaining values.

**2.3 Row 16 is a claw.** `W16 = sigma1(W14) + W9 + sigma0(W1) + W0`. With `a1 = u + h(a0, cv)` (`recover_a1`) one finds `W16 - g = F(u) - F(a0)` (mod 2^32), where `g = 0xbca1c321` is the published `W16`, `F(x) = F_even(x)` for even `x` (a 32-bit expression in `x`, `S0(x)`, `MAJ`, `S1`, `CH` and six constants of the first block: `consts`, `fstep`) and `F(x) = sigma0(x) - x` for odd `x`. So `W16 = g` exactly when `F(a0) = F(u)` with `a0` even and `u` odd: a claw of the map `F` on 32-bit words (checked in `r39-transplant-claw`, `w16_identity`). Claws are found by a van Oorschot-Wiener walk (Section 3).

**2.4 What remains to pay, after a claw.** (i) `W8 in F8` and `W9 in F9`, where `F8 = { w : sigma0(w + D8) - sigma0(w) = 0x087ff000 }`, `F9 = { w : sigma0(w + D9) - sigma0(w) = 0x03010000 }` (`inF`); both sets depend only on 14 and 17 active bits of `w`, with exactly 1152 / 2^14 = 9/128 and 2080 / 2^17 = 65/4096 members (enumerated; the active-bit masks `0xCC19E60C` and `0xF9983C0F`), so `p8 p9 = 585 / 524288 = 2^-9.807707`. (ii) `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`, the published `W17` (the program tests it on every claw): probability 2^-32 for a uniform `W17`; the entry needs only that this value works (`solve_row_exact` in `r39-transplant-claw` trial 0 verifies `|G16| = 2`, `|G17(0xbca1c121)| = 0`, and `|G17(g)| = 1` with `G17(g) = {0x71e9ba98}`). (iii) The tail: rows 18 to 38 (`W18..W23` are the six free words of the bijection) must end with equal states after step 38: probability `p_tail`, Section 8.

Events (i) to (iii), together with `W16 = g` and the parity `a0` even, `u` odd, imply the collision: the pair then follows the published trail through row 17 and its remaining rows hold with the tail event. The program ends by compressing the pair and comparing (`Attack.claw`, last block). (The advice pair's own `A[4]` is odd, so `r39-transplant-claw` trial 0 checks the claw equation through the even-branch formula of `F` and passes the pair as `claw(u, a0)` with check mode off.)

## 3. The program

One file, `experiments/r39joint.py` (sections [1] to [5]; the generator source is the string `GEN_C`, printed by `--gen-c`). Per first block `M0` the program runs one WINDOW of `7 x 2^24 = 117,440,512` walk evaluations (`P.WIN`, `Attack.run_window`):

1. Two random 256-bit words give `M0`; one target compression gives `cv`. **Window selection (this entry, `Attack.prefilter` and `P.PFT`, `P.KCAP`, `P.PFTRY`, `P.KTRY`).** A counted loop over the 32 bit positions computes `s = 96 + 8 popcount(cv4 ^ cv5) - 3 popcount(cv0 ^ cv1)` (two XORs and two moves, then per bit two ANDs, two left shifts, two right shifts, two adds, two subtracts and a 2-operation loop branch, then a 2-operation compare and branch, exactly as executed by `Mach`: 390 operations per block; static block `w_pf`, 18 instructions); a block with `s > 96 + 19` is rejected (charged, a new block is drawn: 2 + 16 + 2812 + 390 operations plus 3 for the rejection counter and its cap test); after at most `PFTRY = 2^16` rejections the window would be skipped (probability below `exp(-2^16 x 0.005)`, i.e. never in practice; the run counts it as a window with no claws). A block that passes goes to the generator (step 2); its image must have at most `KCAP = 680` records, otherwise the generation is charged (its executed generator operators, at most the 575,000-operator cap, plus the 140-operation constants `consts` it was given and 2 for the try counter) and a new block is drawn (at most `KTRY = 64` generations per window); every generation also pays 6 operations for its three cap comparisons (`KCAP`, `GCAP`, `RCAP`). Over the 24 measured windows the selection drew 10,246 rejected blocks and ran 56 rejected generations in all (427 and 2.33 per window); its whole cost, with the accepted generation, is the window-setup and generator rows of Section 5 (30.8 operations per claw). The prefilter coefficients and the two thresholds were chosen once from the generator's record counts on 220,000 random first blocks (a participant-side count of `make_kernels_folded` images, no claw search), minimizing expected records plus selection cost. The six constants of `F` (`consts`, 140 operations). When a chaining value is given explicitly (the advice check of `r39-transplant-claw` trial 0) its kernel is used at the first generation without the selection.
2. The generator `GEN_C` (freestanding C, compiled to WebAssembly, Appendix B) runs inside the program and emits the bank A to bank B program of the 256-lane walk step, between 666 and 680 records of 5 bytes in the measured windows (at most 680 by selection), with the five constants of the first block folded in; the bank B to bank A program is the same records with the bank bits of every register field swapped (a relocation pass, 8 operations per record). A first block whose image has more than `KCAP = 680` records (selection, step 1), needs more than 206 registers, or whose generator runs over 575,000 operators is skipped and charged (`Attack.window_setup`). The executed-operator count of `GEN_C` is the full cost of producing the window's kernel: the construction of the data-flow graph, the register allocation, and the emission of the 5-byte records are each steps of `GEN_C`, and every one of its operators is charged (Section 4), inside `m.o` and the work budget of Section 7. The Python routine `make_kernels_folded` of section [1] is only a readable reference that produces byte-identical records (Appendix B: 200 first blocks, both banks; the program asserts the equality in check mode); its `to_source`/`exec` step (compiling a Python function) has no counterpart in the machine model, which executes the 5-byte records directly, charged per record per iteration in stages 1 and 2 (steps 4-5). No construction step is left uncharged.
3. The table is cleared (4 operations per 32-byte word); 256 trails start in the 256 lanes, trail `i` at `x0 = i`.
4. Stage 1 (`Attack.iter1`): `7 x 2^16 = 458,752` iterations of the kernel (one record = XOR, AND, OR or NOT of two 256-bit registers into a third: bit `j` of the 256 words of the lanes lives in plane `j`, rotations are renaming). A distinguished point (DP) is a new point whose low 7 bits are zero; the kernel ends with the OR of the low 7 planes. On a DP (`Attack.hit`): the lane is found (binary search); the DP lanes of the iteration are grouped (change (6)) and the 25 high bits `r` of all lanes of a group are extracted by one Horner pass over the planes with the group's mask; the bucket is `k = floor(r * 171008 / 2^25)` (`171008 = 668 x 2^8`, `668 = 2^9 + 2^7 + 2^5 - 2^2`: four shifts, two adds and a subtract; change (9)) and its 39-bit record `chk(8) | len(11) | id(20)`, `chk = r mod 256`, starts at bit `39 k` of the 26,052-word table (a record that crosses a word boundary takes a second load and, when written, a second store; this entry's change (3)); a bucket holds at most 197 consecutive values of `r`, so (bucket, chk) determines `r` and there is no false match. The record is overwritten except when a MATCH finds a stored trail at least as long as the new one (keep-longer, change (1)), and a hit that does not MATCH writes only if its trail has at least `LMIN = 32` steps (short-trail skip, change (4)); `check` mode asserts `len < 2048` and the bucket formula on every hit. An entry with the same `chk` and a nonzero length is a MATCH (two trails end at one DP): the pair of (id, len) goes into a queue of 3,584 pairs. The lane receives a new trail (next id; the ids of a group's lanes are written plane by plane by one shared pass that XORs each lane's 20-bit delta against its known value `r x 2^7` into planes 0 to 19 and clears planes 20 to 31, changes (6) and (8)). A trail older than 1536 steps is cut by a scan every 512 iterations (`MAXS = 1536`, `CUTP = 512`), so every trail is shorter than 2,048 steps.
5. Stage 2 (`Attack.stage2`), when the queue holds QFULL = 3328 pairs and at the end of the window (an iteration pushes at most one pair per lane, so the queue never holds more than 3327 + 256 = 3583 < QCAP = 3584 pairs: a constant inequality, no probability): pairs run in lane pairs `(p, p + 128)` (128 slots, continuous refill), and the longer trail of a pair whose length difference is at least `D0 = 82` first runs alone in any free lane (change (5): a 256-bit occupancy mask; a finishing catch-up whose partner lane is busy is moved, 4 operations per plane, into a fully free lane pair from a packed free-slot stack; launches that need a fully free lane pair wait while fewer than `KRES = 6` are free; with none free the pair is dropped). When every queued pair has been launched, a call that is not the window's last one parks (this entry's change (2)): the in-flight pairs stay in their lanes, the current stage-2 bank is swapped with the 32-word save area that holds the stage-1 bank (a counted loop of 32 x (2 loads + 2 stores + 3 loop) + 3 = 483 operations; it is the existing save area), the six scalars `cur`, `it2`, `bact`, `live`, `occ`, `nf` are stored (18 operations), and the next stage-2 call tests the parked flag (2), loads them (18) and swaps the banks back (483) and continues with the pairs where they were (a call that finds nothing parked saves the stage-1 bank, and a final call restores it: 291 operations each; a call that finds nothing parked also initializes the six scalars and the 4-word free-slot stack, 22 operations in block `s2_init`, and every call sets its queue cursors, 3 operations in block `s2_q`; the timing wheel is empty after every drain, which check mode asserts, so it is never re-zeroed, and a lane record is always written before it is read); the timing wheel (2,048 buckets: a pair's length difference is at most 2,047), the lane records and the free-slot stack stay as they are. The last call of a window (after the 458,752 iterations) runs until every pair has merged. The longer trail starts at its start point, the shorter one `len` difference iterations later (a timing wheel); after each iteration the two states of a pair are compared (12 planes first, the other 20 only for candidates); the first iteration with equal states gives the predecessors `(a, b)`. Opposite parity is a claw (`a0` even, `u` odd). Every lane insert of stage 2 is queued and the queue is written by one shared pass (change (7)) before any lane move, kernel step, park or drain.
6. Claw filters (`Attack.claw`, exact, scalar, every operation counted): `a1 = sigma0(u) + a0 + const` (uses the claw equation), `W9 = c9 - a1 in F9`; then `W8 in F8`; then `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`. A claw that passes all three is built into the pair `(M1, M1')`; both are compressed (2 target compressions, charged) and compared; a collision ends the run.

The attack is the loop over first blocks. In `--attack` the 16 words of each window's first block are drawn from the operating system's random source (`random.SystemRandom`, i.e. `os.urandom`): fresh algorithmic coins for every window, so the windows are independent trials of the algorithm (H1 part (a) is about the hash, not about the coins); `--seed S` exists only to make the experiments and the `--windows` measurements reproducible and is not used by the program. Its halts are the window count `B` and the charged-work budget `W_BUDGET` (Section 7), a skipped image (charged), and success; if a window's trail counter `idc` reaches 2^20 (trail ids are 20 bits), the `Attack.hit` or `Attack.cutscan` branch that finds no free id sets the stage-1 limit register to 0 (1 operation, on that path only; no per-iteration test is added), so stage 1 ends after the current iteration and the window's final stage 2 drains the queue and any parked pairs (`r39-window-small` trial 4 forces this path; across the 24 full windows of `7 x 2^24` evaluations `idc` ends at 912,442 to 921,520, under 0.879 x 2^20, so the stop never triggered in a measured window).

## 4. Machine model and prices (declared once)

A counted word RAM of 256-bit words with 256 registers (8,192 bytes, counted as memory), the cost model collision-frontier-v5: the unit is the target compression, 2812 operations (`C` of `sha256-r39`). Charged, one operation each: XOR, AND, OR, NOT, move, shift, rotate, add, subtract, compare and set; a conditional branch is a compare and a branch (2); a load or a store is 3 (index shift, base add, access); a call and its return are 2. A main-loop body record (register-to-register XOR, AND, OR, NOT, no immediate operand) is 5 bytes (an opcode and three 10-bit register fields), the price of dariolina's 6b678689 and of Jbenisek's 0c1ef7c3; every other instruction is 36 bytes. One executed WebAssembly operator of the generator is one charged operation (generator halts at 575,000 operators); this count is the whole kernel-construction cost -- the data-flow graph build, the register allocation and the 5-byte record emission performed by `GEN_C` -- and not a surrogate for it, since `GEN_C` and the Python reference `make_kernels_folded` emit identical records (Appendix B). These are prices of the cost model; they are not probabilistic premises. Every operation of the program goes through the class `Mach` of the program (one method = one charged primitive, `m.o` counts them); there is no averaged price anywhere in the ledger.

parking only removes partly filled iterations, and a parked pair resumes in the next call, still at most `MAXS + CUTP` iterations long), each iteration at most 2^20.1 operations (kernel, launches, at most 256 catch-up completions with their lane moves, compares, and at most 128 claw filters with their verification; with the batched inserts of change (7) summed at their census block maxima: at most 256 completions of at most 401 operations including a flush, 256 launches of at most 381, 128 merges of at most 6,333 including the full claw verification, the kernel and the fixed blocks, about 1.01 x 10^6 < 2^20); and at most `PFTRY = 2^16` rejected first blocks (each at most 3,300 operations) and `KTRY = 64` kernel generations (each at most 575,000 + 148 operations, plus 8 x 680 relocation for the accepted one), all charged (a window that exhausts either cap is skipped); together under `2^28`. Every recorded trail length is below 2,048 (so the 11-bit length fields and the 2,048-bucket wheel never overflow) because a trail is either restarted by a cut scan once it is `MAXS = 1536` iterations old, the scans `CUTP = 512` iterations apart, or, once no trail id is left, stage 1 stops at `idc = 2^20` and records nothing further; this bound holds in every run, not only when `idc` stays below 2^20. Hence the charged work of one window is at most `(2^30.87 + 2^26.11) x 2^20.1 + 2^28 + 2^39 < 2^51.1 <= R = 2^52` operations (this entry uses `R = 2^52`), and its number of distinct claws and groups is at most `7 x 2^24`.

## 7. Success probability and time

`p_claw = p8 p9 2^-32 p_tail`, `log2 p_claw` = -116.297707 (with `log2 p_tail` = -74.49, Section 8). **Claws are counted by groups.** Two claws of a window can depend on each other only if they share `a0` or `u` (a value with three or more preimages under `F`); grouping the claws of a window into connected components by shared endpoint (`count_groups`), one claw per group is enough for the event, and distinct groups have disjoint inputs `(a0, u)` of the bijection. Over the 24 full windows (Appendix A), there are `2,647,997` distinct claw groups in `2,653,807` distinct claws: per-window mean `110333.21` groups (standard deviation `330.36`, one-sided 99% Student-t lower bound `g- = 110,164.62` groups/window) and group-to-claw ratio `2,647,997 / 2,653,807 = 0.997811` (per-window mean `0.997811`, standard deviation `0.000098`, one-sided 99% Student-t lower bound `r- = 0.99776`, giving `c- r- = 110,405.10 x 0.99776 = 110,157.79 <= g-`). Conditional on producing at least `G` distinct groups, the run succeeds with probability `1 - (1 - p_claw)^G >= 1 - exp(-G p_claw)`. To absorb the Hoeffding failure probability with an explicit positive margin above `0.39`, we set `G p_claw = lambda = -ln(0.60999) = 0.494313`, so `1 - exp(-G p_claw) = 0.390010`, `log2 G` = 115.2812, and with the 99% lower bound `r- = 0.99776`, `N = G / r- = 2^115.2844` distinct claws. Over the distinct claws (the trials the claim counts) of the 24 windows of this entry, the F9 and F8 counts are binomial from window to window (chi-square 24.9 and 38.2 on 24 degrees of freedom against the exact rates 65/4096 and 9/128 (the F8 value has p = 0.033, a mild overdispersion that a chaining-value dependent F8 rate could produce; the pooled F8 rate itself matches the exact 9/128, and the claim uses the exact rates of the constructions' enumerated sets, premise (c)); pooled 41,962 / 2,653,807 = 0.015812 against 65/4096 = 0.015869, z = -0.74, which the claim does not use (it takes the exact 65/4096, premise (c)), and `W8 in F8` 186,791 / 2,653,807 = 0.07039 against 9/128 = 0.07031, z = 0.47; both 2,925 against 2961.1 expected). The lag-1 autocorrelation of the F9 indicator over the distinct claws in the program's claw order is within 0.0053 in every window (mean -0.0006; 641 adjacent F9 pairs against 663.9 expected under independence; this count is not used by the claim: a pairwise dependence between claws enters the success probability only through joint terms of order `G p_claw^2 ~ 2^-116`, which are negligible). At the lower bound `c- = 110,405.10` distinct claws per window (`c- r- = 110,157.79 <= g- = 110,164.62`), with the margin `(1 + 2^-19)`, this is `B = floor(N (1 + 2^-19) / c-) + 1 = 458232275784239789566322955690 = 2^98.5320` windows (first blocks).

**Total work and total groups/claws (Hoeffding).** The windows are independent trials (fresh coins per window, H1 part (a)), each with charged work in `[0, R]` (`R = 2^52`, Section 5), distinct claws in `[0, 7 x 2^24]`, and distinct claw groups in `[0, 7 x 2^24]`. By Hoeffding's inequality the total work of `B` windows exceeds `B mu + B mu+ 2^-20` (and so in particular `B mu+ (1 + 2^-20)`, as `mu <= mu+`) with probability at most `exp(-2 B (mu+ 2^-20)^2 / R^2) <= exp(-2^14.9)`, the total number of distinct claws falls below `B c (1 - 2^-20) >= N` with probability at most `exp(-2^39.4)`, and the total number of distinct claw groups falls below `B g (1 - 2^-20) >= B g- (1 - 2^-20) >= B c- r- (1 - 2^-20) >= G` with probability at most `exp(-2^39.4)` (`mu, c, g` the true means; `mu <= mu+`, `c >= c-` and `g >= g-` is premise H1 part (b)). So with probability at least `1 - 2 exp(-2^14.9)` all `B` windows run inside the budget and produce at least `G` distinct claw groups (and at least `N` distinct claws).

**The budget.** `--attack` stops at a success, after `B_WINDOWS` windows, or as soon as its charged work reaches `W_BUDGET = floor(B x 874,983,329 x (1 + 2^-20)) + 1 = 400945984492463235588962129906188838254 = 2^128.2367` operations (checked before each window, 6 operations per window), whichever comes first, so its charged work is at most `W_BUDGET + R + 6 B` in every run. The time is

`T = (W_BUDGET + R + 6 B) / 2812` target-compressions (`log2(T + 2^36) = 116.77929`), `time_log2` = 116.780 (exact 116.77929, rounded up), against 116.865 for 651ada7 with the same tail and advice; by the union bound, the unconditional success probability is at least `(1 - 0.60999) - 2 exp(-2^14.9) > 0.390009 > 0.39`.

## 11. Advice and preprocessing

Preprocessing (`H3-advice-construction`, the form of sraiw 38912d66 Section 4.2, credited). The program reads from the advice only the dense part of the published characteristic: the state words `a`, `e` of steps 2 to 15 of both blocks, the message words `W10..W15`, the differences `D8`, `D9`, the row-16 value `g` and the row-17 value `0x71e9ba98`; every word after row 17 is a fresh draw of the run, paid online by the 2^-32 of row 17 and by `p_tail` (Sections 2.4, 7 and 8). (2) Each candidate is tested for a non-empty row-16 fibre `G16` and row-17 fibre `G17(g)` by exact 32-bit bit-by-bit DFS (`solve_row_exact` in `r39joint.py`, using `ROW_MASKS_16_17` and `CONDS_16_17`).
