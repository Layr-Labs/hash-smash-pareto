Target `sha256-r39-prefix-v1`. Claim: `time_log2` = 118.345 target-compressions, `memory_log2_bytes` = 20 (1,047,892 bytes, the 160-byte advice included), success probability at least 0.39, `preprocessing_log2` = 36 (Section 11, a count), advice 160 bytes.

The word-RAM cost convention comes from dariolina's 744875ec and our 3c1d7f68, 368d1718, 01e31be9; the 5-byte record convention is dariolina's 6b678689 and our 0c1ef7c3. ## 1. Target and output

SHA-256 with 39 steps in every compression, FIPS 180-4 padding, the standard IV, feed-forward after step 38, all 256 digest bits. The output is two distinct 128-byte messages `M0 || M1` and `M0 || M1'`: the same first block, second blocks that give the same chaining value after the second compression; the padding block that follows is identical, so the two digests are equal. The first block `M0` is a random block, `cv = compress39(IV, M0)`.

## 2. The construction (sraiw 38912d66, dariolina)

**2.1 The base is advice.** The pair of ePrint 2026/1120, Table 23, is a 39-step semi-free-start collision: a chaining value `CV_PUB` (8 words), two blocks `M'` and `M` (16 words each), 160 bytes, printed in `experiments/r39joint.py` section [1] (`CV_PUB`, `M_PUB`, `MP_PUB`). It is a collision of the 39-step compression from `CV_PUB` (experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trial 0; our `compress39` equals the organizer's `verifier/hash_functions.py` on 300 random blocks, and the published pair collides under the organizer reference). It is the advice of this entry. Its trajectories `BA, BE` (message `M'`) and `CA, CE` (message `M`) are expanded from the 160 bytes by two compressions at the start of the run (charged 2 x 2812 + 120 operations, `Attack.base_setup`).

**2.2 The transplant.** Write `A[r]` and `E[r]` for the two state registers after step `r-1`, `a0 = A[4]`, `a1 = A[5]`, `u = W1`. Fix `A[6..9]`, `E[10..13]` and `W10..W15` to the published values of `M'`. For any chaining value `cv` and any `(a0, a1)`, steps 0 to 9 then determine `W0..W9` uniquely (`build(cv, a0, a1)` in section [1]); the partner message takes `W8 + D8`, `W9 + D9` and the published `W10..W15` of `M` (`second`). Both messages then follow the published trajectories `A[6..19]`, `E[10..19]` (steps 0 to 15 only, an identity of the step function, not a probability). The map `(cv, a0, a1) <-> (W8, W9, W16..W23)` is a bijection (320 bits, triangular). Experiment `r39-transplant-claw`, trials 1 to 255, checks the identity on 255 random chaining values.

**2.3 Row 16 is a claw.** `W16 = sigma1(W14) + W9 + sigma0(W1) + W0`. With `a1 = u + h(a0, cv)` (`recover_a1`) one finds `W16 - g = F(u) - F(a0)` (mod 2^32), where `g = 0xbca1c321` is the published `W16`, `F(x) = F_even(x)` for even `x` (a 32-bit expression in `x`, `S0(x)`, `MAJ`, `S1`, `CH` and six constants of the first block: `consts`, `fstep`) and `F(x) = sigma0(x) - x` for odd `x`. So `W16 = g` exactly when `F(a0) = F(u)` with `a0` even and `u` odd: a claw of the map `F` on 32-bit words (checked in `r39-transplant-claw`, `w16_identity`). Claws are found by a van Oorschot-Wiener walk (Section 3).

**2.4 What remains to pay, after a claw.** (i) `W8 in F8` and `W9 in F9`, where `F8 = { w : sigma0(w + D8) - sigma0(w) = 0x087ff000 }`, `F9 = { w : sigma0(w + D9) - sigma0(w) = 0x03010000 }` (`inF`); both sets depend only on 14 and 17 active bits of `w`, with exactly 1152 / 2^14 = 9/128 and 2080 / 2^17 = 65/4096 members (enumerated; the active-bit masks `0xCC19E60C` and `0xF9983C0F`), so `p8 p9 = 585 / 524288 = 2^-9.807707`. (ii) `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`, the published `W17` (the program tests it on every claw): probability 2^-32 for a uniform `W17`; the entry needs only that this value works (the exhaustive counts `|G16| = 2` and `|G17(g)| = 1` of 38912d66 and 2c48b18c say no other `W17` is available; the second `W16` value is not used). (iii) The tail: rows 18 to 38 (`W18..W23` are the six free words of the bijection) must end with equal states after step 38: probability `p_tail`, Section 8.

Events (i) to (iii), together with `W16 = g` and the parity `a0` even, `u` odd, imply the collision: the pair then follows the published trail through row 17 and its remaining rows hold with the tail event. The program ends by compressing the pair and comparing (`Attack.claw`, last block).

## 3. The program

One file, `experiments/r39joint.py` (sections [1] to [5]; the generator source is the string `GEN_C`, printed by `--gen-c`). Per first block `M0` the program runs one WINDOW of 2^26 walk evaluations (`Attack.run_window`):

1. Two random 256-bit words give `M0`; one target compression gives `cv`; the six constants of `F` (`consts`, 140 operations).
2. The generator `GEN_C` (freestanding C, compiled to WebAssembly, Appendix B) runs inside the program and emits the bank A to bank B program of the 256-lane walk step, about 723 records of 5 bytes, with the five constants of the first block folded in; the bank B to bank A program is the same records with the bank bits of every register field swapped (a relocation pass, 8 operations per record). A first block whose image has more than 780 records, needs more than 206 registers, or whose generator runs over 575,000 operators is skipped and charged (`Attack.window_setup`). The executed-operator count of `GEN_C` is the full cost of producing the window's kernel: the construction of the data-flow graph, the register allocation, and the emission of the 5-byte records are each steps of `GEN_C`, and every one of its operators is charged (Section 4), inside `m.o` and the work budget of Section 7. The Python routine `make_kernels_folded` of section [1] is only a readable reference that produces byte-identical records (Appendix B: 200 first blocks, both banks; the program asserts the equality in check mode); its `to_source`/`exec` step (compiling a Python function) has no counterpart in the machine model, which executes the 5-byte records directly, charged per record per iteration in stages 1 and 2 (steps 4-5). No construction step is left uncharged.
3. The table is cleared (4 operations per 32-byte word); 256 trails start in the 256 lanes, trail `i` at `x0 = i`.
4. Stage 1 (`Attack.iter1`): 2^18 iterations of the kernel (one record = XOR, AND, OR or NOT of two 256-bit registers into a third: bit `j` of the 256 words of the lanes lives in plane `j`, rotations are renaming). A distinguished point (DP) is a new point whose low 7 bits are zero; the kernel ends with the OR of the low 7 planes. On a DP (`Attack.hit`): the lane is found (binary search), the 25 high bits `r` are extracted by a Horner pass over the planes, the slot is `r * 26112 words >> 25` split into word and sub-slot, the 5-byte entry is `chk(8) | len(12) | id(20)` with `chk = r mod 256`, 6 entries per 32-byte word, always overwrite; so (slot, chk) determines `r` and there is no false match. An entry with the same `chk` and a nonzero length is a MATCH (two trails end at one DP): the pair of (id, len) goes into a queue of 4096 pairs. The lane receives a new trail (next id, inserted plane by plane). A trail older than 2048 steps is cut by a scan every 1024 iterations.
5. Stage 2 (`Attack.stage2`), when the queue holds QFULL = 3840 pairs and at the end of the window (an iteration pushes at most one pair per lane, so the queue never holds more than 3839 + 256 = 4095 < QCAP = 4096 pairs: a constant inequality, no probability): pairs run in lane pairs `(p, p + 128)`, 128 slots, continuous refill. The longer trail starts at its start point, the shorter one `len` difference iterations later (a timing wheel); after each iteration the two states of a pair are compared (12 planes first, the other 20 only for candidates); the first iteration with equal states gives the predecessors `(a, b)`. Opposite parity is a claw (`a0` even, `u` odd).
6. Claw filters (`Attack.claw`, exact, scalar, every operation counted): `a1 = sigma0(u) + a0 + const` (uses the claw equation), `W9 = c9 - a1 in F9`; then `W8 in F8`; then `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`. A claw that passes all three is built into the pair `(M1, M1')`; both are compressed (2 target compressions, charged) and compared; a collision ends the run.

The attack is the loop over first blocks. In `--attack` the 16 words of each window's first block are drawn from the operating system's random source (`random.SystemRandom`, i.e. `os.urandom`): fresh algorithmic coins for every window, so the windows are independent trials of the algorithm (H1 part (a) is about the hash, not about the coins); `--seed S` exists only to make the experiments and the `--windows` measurements reproducible and is not used by the program. Its halts are the window count B (Section 7), a skipped image (charged), an exhausted id space (a window creates one trail per DP hit, about 525,000, below the 2^20 ids of the entry layout), and success.

## 4. Machine model and prices (declared once)

A counted word RAM of 256-bit words with 256 registers (8,192 bytes, counted as memory), the cost model collision-frontier-v5: the unit is the target compression, 2812 operations (`C` of `sha256-r39`). Charged, one operation each: XOR, AND, OR, NOT, move, shift, rotate, add, subtract, compare and set; a conditional branch is a compare and a branch (2); a load or a store is 3 (index shift, base add, access); a call and its return are 2. A main-loop body record (register-to-register XOR, AND, OR, NOT, no immediate operand) is 5 bytes (an opcode and three 10-bit register fields), the price of dariolina's 6b678689 and of our 0c1ef7c3; every other instruction is 36 bytes. One executed WebAssembly operator of the generator is one charged operation (generator halts at 575,000 operators); this count is the whole kernel-construction cost -- the data-flow graph build, the register allocation and the 5-byte record emission performed by `GEN_C` -- and not a surrogate for it, since `GEN_C` and the Python reference `make_kernels_folded` emit identical records (Appendix B). These are prices of the cost model; they are not probabilistic premises. Every operation of the program goes through the class `Mach` of the program (one method = one charged primitive, `m.o` counts them); there is no averaged price anywhere in the ledger.

**Every window has a worst-case cost (R).** Every loop of a window is bounded by a constant of the program: 2^18 stage-1 iterations, each at most 787 operations of kernel and loop plus at most 256 DP handlers (straight-line code apart from one loop over the 25 weight digits; under 4,096 operations each) and the cut scan; a trail is at most `MAXS + CUTP = 3,072` iterations long (a trail older than 2,048 is cut at the next scan, every 1,024 iterations), so the at most 2^26 pairs of a window (one per DP hit) take at most 2^26 x 3,072 pair-iterations, i.e. at most 2^30.6 full stage-2 iterations of 128 pairs plus at most 3,072 partly filled iterations per stage-2 call (at most 2^14.02 calls), each iteration at most 2^20.1 operations (kernel, launches, compares, and at most 128 claw filters with their verification); and at most `KTRY = 8` kernel generations, each charged (a window whose 8 generations all fail is skipped). Hence the charged work of one window is at most `R = 2^51` operations, and its number of distinct claws is at most 2^26.

## 7. Success probability and time

`p_claw = p8 p9 2^-32 p_tail`, `log2 p_claw` = -117.4777 (with `log2 p_tail` = -75.67, Section 8). **Claws are counted by groups.** Two claws of a window can depend on each other only if they share `a0` or `u` (a value with three or more preimages under `F`); grouping the claws of a window by shared endpoint, one claw per group is enough for the event, and distinct groups have disjoint inputs `(a0, u)` of the bijection. The run succeeds with probability `1 - (1 - p_claw)^G >= 1 - exp(-G p_claw)` for `G` groups; for probability at least 0.39, `G p_claw = lambda = -ln(0.61) = 0.494296`, so `log2 G` = 116.4612, and with the measured ratio of groups to distinct claws, 303,550 / 304,000 = 0.99858 (6 full windows), `log2 N` = 116.4632 distinct claws. The F9 and F8 counts are binomial from window to window (chi-square 22.6 and 32.0 on 24 degrees of freedom; 4.8 and 5.3 on 6 for 6 more windows) and the lag-1 autocorrelation of the F9 indicator in discovery order is within 0.012. At the lower bound `c- = 50,472.66` distinct claws per window, with a margin `(1 + 2^-19)`, this is `B = floor(N (1 + 2^-19) / c-) + 1 = 2^100.8400` windows (first blocks).

**Total work and total claws (Hoeffding).** The windows are independent trials (fresh coins per window, H1 part (a)), each with charged work in `[0, R]` and distinct claws in `[0, 2^26]`. By Hoeffding's inequality the total work of B windows exceeds `B (mu + mu 2^-20)` with probability at most `exp(-2 B (mu 2^-20)^2 / R^2) <= exp(-2^17.9)`, and the total number of distinct claws falls below `B c (1 - 2^-20)` with probability at most `exp(-2^40.8)` (mu, c the true means; `mu <= mu+` and `c >= c-` is premise H1 part (b)). So with probability at least `1 - 2 exp(-2^17.9)` all B windows run inside the budget and produce at least N distinct claws.

**The budget.** `--attack` stops at a success, after `B_WINDOWS` windows, or as soon as its charged work reaches `W_BUDGET = floor(B x 523,024,049 x (1 + 2^-20)) + 1 = 2^129.8023` operations (checked before each window, 6 operations per window), whichever comes first, so its charged work is at most `W_BUDGET + R + 6 B` in every run. The time is

`T = (W_BUDGET + R + 6 B) / 2812` target-compressions, `time_log2` = 118.345 (exact 118.34492, rounded up), against 118.3310 at the plain means; the success probability is at least `0.39 - 2 exp(-2^17.9)`, i.e. 0.39 at any printed precision, because B carries the margin `(1 + 2^-19)`.

On a success `--attack` prints the two messages; otherwise it prints the number of windows and the charged work and exits with status 1. Each 1 bit of `log2 p_tail` moves `time_log2` by 1 bit: at `log2 p_tail` = -75.70 the time is 118.3749, at -75.34 it is 118.0149, at -77.41 it is 120.0849.

## 8. The tail p_tail (rows 18 to 38)

Definition. Distribution `U`: `E16`, `E17` of the first member pinned to the published values (this fixes `W16 = g`, `W17 = 0x71e9ba98`); `W8` uniform on `F8`, `W9` uniform on `F9`, independent; `E18..E23` of the first member independent and uniform on 2^32 (`W18..W23` are a bijective image of them); everything else is determined by the real step function and message expansion. `Q = P_U(collision and K_P | a0 even, u odd) = 4 P_U(collision and K_P and a0 even and u odd)`, where the collision test is exact (equal 256-bit outputs of the two 39-step compressions) and `K_P` is an importance carrier (the printed cells of Tables 21 and 22 of rows 16 to 18, signed differences only for rows 19 to 22, `E23[30] = 0`). `K_P` is a subset of all routes to a collision, so `Q <= p_tail` and the cost bound uses `p_tail >= 2^q_tail` with `q_tail` = -75.67.

Declared value. `q_tail` = -75.67. Evidence: the replica `experiments/r39tail.py` runs in the organizer sandbox as `r39-tail-01` to `r39-tail-14` (Section 10). Each run estimates `Q'` (this event without the parity condition) as an SMC estimate of the carrier `K_P` over rows 18 to 23 times the fraction of proposals with equal 256-bit states after step 38 (`W8`, `W9` redrawn in `F8`, `F9` for every proposal); every hit is rebuilt into a chaining value and checked by the two 39-step compressions. Our run of the 14 entries with the organizer's public seeds: 601 hits in 8,601,600 proposals, all 601 verified collisions; mean log2 Q' = -75.393, one-sided 99% Student lower bound over the 14 runs -75.592. The declared -75.67 lies 0.08 bit below that bound, so that a fresh draw of the 14 runs covers it with probability about 0.95. Cross-checks: an offline C run of the same estimator (64 seeds x 2^28 samples, 1.26M collisions) gives -75.354 (bound -75.370) and the parity ratio Q / Q' = 1.0033; sraiw's published bound for this event is -75.4278 (38912d66).

## 9. Premises (declared in claim.json)

`H1-claw-model` (score-critical), four parts. (a) The chaining value `cv` of a random first block behaves as an independent random value for the walk map and the later events, so the windows are independent trials. (b) The program's yield and cost per window are those measured on the real step function over 24 full-size windows (50,567.9 distinct claws, 10,263.1 operations per claw); the program cannot be run for 2^100.8 windows. (c) A distinct claw is, for the events `W8 in F8`, `W9 in F9`, `W17 = published` and the tail, a trial with the probabilities of Sections 2.4 and 8, independent of the walk's yield. (d) `W17` is uniform over the claws.

Test of (c) and (d), on claws of the real walk (our test script, not shipped; 310,224 distinct claws from 6 random first blocks, found by our C model of the same walk, whose stage-1 and stage-2 counts agree with the program to 0.1 percent; each with `F(a0) = F(u)`, `a0` even, `u` odd, `W16 = g` verified against the construction): `W9 in F9` 5,015 (0.01617; expected 0.01587); `W8 in F8` 22,002 (0.07092; 0.07031); both 360 (0.001160; 0.001116). Matches of `W17` with the published value in the low `k` bits (expected `n 2^-k`): k = 8: 1,199 (1,211.8); k = 12: 82 (75.7); k = 16: 5 (4.73); k = 20: 0 (0.30); k = 24: 0 (0.02). Chi-square of the low 16 bits of `W17` against uniform: 65,830.2 with 65,535 degrees of freedom, z = 0.82. The test covers the low 16 bits of `W17` and the side-condition rates; the high 16 bits of `W17`, the independence of `W17` from the tail event and the full 2^-32 are not testable at this sample size and are the premise (d).

`H2-tail` (score-critical): `p_tail >= 2^q_tail` for claws uniform on the fibre, Section 8. `H5-group-independence` (score-critical): distinct claw groups (disjoint inputs `(a0, u)` of the bijection, within a window and across windows) are independent trials for the events `W8 in F8`, `W9 in F9`, `W17 = 0x71e9ba98` and the tail, so the run succeeds with probability `1 - (1 - p_claw)^G` over `G` groups (Section 7). Support: across windows the chaining value is fresh (H1 part (a)); within a window, the F9 and F8 counts are binomial from window to window (chi-square 22.6 and 32.0 on 24 degrees of freedom) and the lag-1 autocorrelation of the F9 indicator in discovery order is within 0.012 (Section 7). Limit: independence of `W17` and of the tail between groups sharing one chaining value is not testable at this sample size and is the premise.

## 11. Advice and preprocessing

Preprocessing, derived (the form of sraiw 38912d66 Section 4.2, credited). The program reads from the advice only the dense part of the published characteristic: the state words `a`, `e` of steps 2 to 15 of both blocks, the message words `W10..W15`, the differences `D8`, `D9`, the row-16 value `g` and the row-17 value `0x71e9ba98`; every word after row 17 is a fresh draw of the run, paid online by the 2^-32 of row 17 and by `p_tail` (Sections 2.4, 7 and 8). The cost of producing that dense part is counted as follows.
